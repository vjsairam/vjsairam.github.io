AI workload cost assessment

Five working days on one AI workload. You find out what each completed task costs today, whether it is worth that, and the cheapest setup that still meets your quality and latency targets.

Who it is for

Teams with an AI feature or agent in production, or close to launch, whose model bill has become a line someone asks about. It works best when the workload has real traffic and one person owns the decision.

What you get

  • A cost baseline: what one completed task costs today, worked out from your bills and a sample of real requests.
  • A quality baseline on your own tasks, with an evaluator you keep.
  • Your options measured on the same tasks against the same targets: tune what you have (routing, caching, prompt size, a smaller managed model), run your own model, or a mix.
  • A recommendation, and the volume or price at which it would change. "Change nothing" is a valid answer, and sometimes the right one.
  • If your own model or a mix comes out ahead: a capacity and reliability plan covering replicas, failover and how fast it can scale.
  • A written report, the cost model and the evidence behind every number, and a walkthrough with your team.

The sample results dashboard shows what the evidence looks like, built from my own benchmark runs.

How the five days run

  • Before day one: we agree the workload, the targets and what I can see. The five days start once the data is in hand.
  • Day 1: cost and quality baseline.
  • Days 2 and 3: the alternatives, measured on your tasks.
  • Day 4: capacity, failure and cost-at-volume checks on the leading option.
  • Day 5: written recommendation and walkthrough.

What I need from you

  • Usage and billing exports for the workload, ideally the last 30 days.
  • A few hundred representative requests with the answers you would accept, redacted as you see fit.
  • Your latency and quality targets, or the person who can set them.
  • If we test running your own model, GPU time in your cloud account, or in mine passed through at cost.

Read-only access is enough. I don't need to touch production, I'm happy to sign an NDA and data processing terms, and your data is deleted at the end.
